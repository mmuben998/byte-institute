Chemistry: The study of the composition, structure, and properties of matter, the processes that matter undergoes, and the energy changes that accompany these processes.

1. The 6 Main Branches of Chemistry

Click cards for context

1. Analytical Chemistry

Identification of the components and composition of materials.

First-Year Example: Testing water to see if it is pure.

2. Organic Chemistry

Study of most carbon-containing compounds.

First-Year Example: Studying plastics, oil, and medicine.

3. Physical Chemistry

Study of properties and changes of matter and their relation to energy.

First-Year Example: Studying how heat changes liquid into steam.

4. Inorganic Chemistry

Study of non-organic compounds (materials without carbon).

First-Year Example: Studying metals, rocks, and minerals.

5. Biochemistry

Study of substances and processes in living things.

First-Year Example: Studying how food digests in the stomach.

6. Theoretical Chemistry

Use of math and computers to understand chemical behavior.

First-Year Example: Using computer models to draw molecules.

2. Matter & Fundamental Units

Matter: Anything that has mass and occupies space.

Atom

The smallest unit of an element that maintains the chemical identity of that element.

Easy Examples:
H (Hydrogen) O (Oxygen) Fe (Iron)

Element

A pure substance that cannot be broken down into simpler, stable substances and is made of only one type of atom.

Easy Examples:
Pure Gold (Au) Iron (Fe) O₂ Gas

Molecule

A substance made of two or more atoms combined that can be broken down into simpler substances.

Easy Examples:
H₂O (Water) CO₂ (Carbon Dioxide)

3. The 4 States of Matter

Solid

Volume: Definite
Shape: Definite
Examples: Ice cube, stone, iron key.

Liquid

Volume: Definite
Shape: Indefinite
Examples: Water, milk, oil (takes container shape).

Gas

Volume: Indefinite
Shape: Indefinite
Examples: Air, oxygen gas, steam (fills container).

Plasma

Volume/Shape: Variable
High-temp state where atoms lose electrons.
Examples: Lightning, sun, neon signs.

4. Properties & Changes in Matter

P

Physical Change

A change that can be observed without changing the identity of the substance.

Simple Examples:
  • Cutting paper
  • Melting ice
  • Boiling water
  • Grinding salt
C

Chemical Change

A change in which substances are converted into new substances (Chemical Reaction).

Reactants: Starting substances that react.
Products: New substances formed.
Simple Examples:
  • Burning wood
  • Rusting iron
  • Baking bread

Chemical Reaction Examples

1. Carbon Burning

C + O₂ ➔ CO₂ ↑

Reactants: C + O₂
Product: CO₂
2. Iron Rusting

4Fe + 3O₂ ➔ 2Fe₂O₃

Reactants: 4Fe + 3O₂
Product: 2Fe₂O₃
3. Electrolysis (Water)

2H₂O ➔ 2H₂ ↑ + O₂ ↑

Reactant: 2H₂O
Products: 2H₂, O₂
